lasteverLM - Frequently asked questions

This file is one of the sources in the "Getting started with lasteverLM"
notebook. Ask the chat about it, or open the handbook PDF beside it for
the complete technical description.


1. What is lasteverLM?

An assistant that turns material into understanding you can check. You
add sources (PDF, text, web pages, images, audio), then ask questions or
choose an output form: report, quiz, data table, chart, mind map,
infographic, spoken audio, or a decision. Every claim in an answer or an
output points to the passage it came from.


2. What does "checkable" mean here?

Every unit of an output (a report paragraph, a table row, a mind-map
node, a sentence of an audio transcript) carries the numbered passages
it rests on. Click a marker and you see the passage word for word, its
page, and how similar it was to the question. Every output also has a
"How this was made" panel: which passages were retrieved, which went
into the prompt, which model wrote it, what it cost, and how the check
came out.


3. What do "supported", "unsupported" and "unverified" mean?

After an output is written, a deterministic check (no second model)
compares each unit with the passages it cites. "Supported": at least
half of its content words appear in its best cited passage.
"Unsupported": they do not. "Unverified": the output was written in
another language than the sources, so word overlap cannot tell. Charts
are checked more strictly: a number counts as supported only if it is
written in the cited passage. Nothing is hidden: a unit that fails is
marked, never silently dropped.


4. What does the judge do, and do I need it?

The word check sees words, not meaning: "the lamp was not electrified
in 1906" passes against "electrified in 1906". The optional judge is a
second model, from another family than the author, that reads each
claim against its passages and says "supported", "contradicted" or "not
in the source". At creation it can revise failing claims, at most three
rounds; a claim that still fails is removed and listed as removed. Three
levels (low, medium, high) differ in model and price. The models were
chosen by a published evaluation, linked in the handbook. Use it when a
wrong sentence would cost you more than the check does.


5. What is a decision?

You ask a question (yes/no, a choice between options, or a scale) and
two things happen independently: the platform's decision model gives a
number, and the language model writes the evidence for and against
from the same passages. Both are checked, and the page tells you
whether they agree. If no evidence on either side holds up, the answer
is "The sources do not decide this."


6. What does it cost?

The public price list shows every price before you sign in:
https://lasteverlm.zehentleitner.co/prices
Before every paid action the app shows you the price. Adding a source
is free; processing it (reading, transcribing, indexing) costs once.
Asking about sources that are already processed costs only the answer.


7. What happens without credit?

You can still add sources; they wait with the status "waiting for
credit" and are processed as soon as you redeem a voucher. Creating
outputs and asking questions need credit.


8. Which files can I add?

PDF (read by its text layer, or by a vision model for scanned pages, or
"auto" for both), plain text and Markdown, web pages by URL, images
(text in the image is transcribed and the image described), and audio:
MP3 and WAV directly, M4A, AAC, OGG, Opus, FLAC, WebM and the sound of
an MP4 converted first. Converted audio can be played and downloaded
beside the original, so you can check what was transcribed.


9. Is a file I upload twice charged twice?

No. Your library stores each file once (recognised by its content, a
URL by its address) and links it into as many notebooks as you like.


10. What happens when I delete something?

Deleting from a notebook only removes the link. Deleting from the
library moves the entry to the trash, where it stays for the number of
days set in Settings (14 by default) and can be restored. Deleting a
notebook asks whether its outputs go too or stay in your library.


11. Can I use an output as a source?

Yes. Outputs live in your library and can be added to any notebook,
where they are searched like any other source.


12. Can others see my files?

No. Nothing is shared between users. When you share an output or a
notebook, the link shows the output with its evidence, and for a
notebook the names of its sources, never the files themselves.


13. Where does my data go?

Everything runs on Vultr: the application in a Kubernetes cluster in
Amsterdam, the database and file storage in Amsterdam. The models
(language, embedding, decision, judge and the speech models) run in
Vultr's US regions, so the text or audio a model reads leaves the EU
for that call; nothing is stored there.


14. Why did my answer say "the sources do not contain this"?

The assistant answers from your selected sources only. If the
retrieved passages do not hold the answer, it says so instead of
guessing. Check which sources are ticked in the Sources list, or ask
more specifically.


15. Why is a source marked "failed"?

The error is shown in the row: a page with no readable text, a web page
that blocks reading, an image without text, or a recording without
speech. A failed source costs only what was already processed.


16. What languages are supported?

The interface speaks English and German. Outputs can be written in
other languages (Output language in the options or in Settings); the
check then marks units as "unverified" where it cannot compare words
across languages.


17. Why do I see "price unavailable"?

The price estimate could not be computed (for example while the
service is restarting). The action still costs credit; the price shows
again on the next try.


18. What does "pricing has no exchange rate" mean?

Model costs are in US dollars and credits in euro. The exchange rate is
taken from the European Central Bank every hour. If it is missing, no
paid call starts at all, so nothing is charged without a rate; sources
wait and run once it is set.


19. Who built this, and how?

One maintainer with seven AI coding agents in three days, every change
as a reviewed pull request that only merged when every check passed,
every decision written down with the alternatives it rejected. The
handbook and the "Keep the Why" dashboard linked from the docs show the
whole history.


20. Can I delete this starter notebook?

Yes. It is your own copy: delete it, rename it, or add your own sources
to it.
