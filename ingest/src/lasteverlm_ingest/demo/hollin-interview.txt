serena: This is Marrow Bay Radio, and I am on the Grey Isles with Tobias Strand, keeper of Hollin Lighthouse. Mr Strand, your grandmother Agathe kept this light before you.
ryan: She did, for thirty-one years, and I have kept it for twenty-two. When she started, the lamp burned colza oil. We changed to paraffin in 1906, and the light has been electric since the spring of 1928.
serena: And the fog signal?
ryan: That is Old Grumble. In fog it sounds twice every fifty seconds, day and night. Fishermen say you can hear it from Saltglass Reef if the wind is kind.
serena: People in town talk about the Strand ledger.
ryan: Twenty-two volumes now. Volume three has the cartographer's ship, the Kestrel, passing on the ninth of May 1893. My grandmother wrote that the lady on deck waved with a theodolite.
serena: How do you live out here?
ryan: I row to Marrow Bay on the first Monday of every month, for paraffin, juniper and news. And every June we count the puffins on Puffin Sunday. Last year it was four thousand and twelve pairs. Brennet.
